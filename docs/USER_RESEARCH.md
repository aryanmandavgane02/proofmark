# User research

## Round 1 survey (5 people, 14 Nov 2026)
College fest organiser, family business owner, startup marketing team, freelance social media manager, student club social media team. Results are in the Round 1 plan (page 2).

## Follow-up questions (16 Nov 2026, same 5 respondents)

| Respondent | Who caught the mistake, and how long did it take? | Suggest fixes or only flag problems? |
|---|---|---|
| College fest organiser | Another committee member noticed the wrong event date after the poster was shared. About 2 hours to correct. | Suggest the fix: show the correct date and let us update it quickly. |
| Family business owner | A customer pointed out an old price in an Instagram post. Noticed after almost a day. | Suggest the fix, especially for prices and product details. |
| Startup marketing team | A teammate noticed an exaggerated claim in a routine review the next morning. | Suggest fixes, but show the source or guideline behind the suggestion. |
| Freelance social media manager | The client caught an incorrect logo after the post went live. About 30 minutes to replace. | Both: first highlight what is wrong, then offer a corrected version I can approve. |
| Student club social media team | A student pointed out a spelling mistake in the comments about 15 minutes after posting. | Definitely suggest fixes; we don't always have someone to proofread. |

**What this tells us**
- 5 of 5 mistakes were caught only after publishing, 3 of 5 by someone outside the team (a customer, the client, a student), between 15 minutes and almost a day later.
- The mistakes match Proofmark's checks: wrong date or old price (fact ledger), exaggerated claim (CLAIM-02), wrong logo (VIS-01), spelling (glossary).
- 5 of 5 want suggested fixes, not just flags. 2 of 5 ask to see the source or rule and to approve the fix themselves, which is how Proofmark's findings and "Accept fix" already work.
